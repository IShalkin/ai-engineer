# Regulated-Domain Controls

Engineering controls for AI in life sciences, healthcare, payments, and financial reporting. They make an obligation satisfiable and evidenced; they never establish that it is satisfied, and none of this is legal advice. The obligation belongs to an accountable function - authorisation holder, covered entity, merchant or acquirer, controller, provider or deployer - not to the system or its engineers. Clause numbers, applicability, and required depth vary by jurisdiction and version in force: confirm every instrument with the accountable function before designing to it.

Provenance per SRC-01. Abbreviations: Part 11 = 21 CFR Part 11; Annex 11 = EudraLex Vol. 4 GMP Annex 11; HIPAA = 45 CFR Part 164; AI Act = Regulation (EU) 2024/1689; ICFR = 17 CFR 240.13a-15. Annex 11 is guidance phrased as "should", not binding clause text; its section numbers anchor an expectation, not an obligation.

## REG-01 - Regulatory Frame

Produce before architecture, not after a compliance review rejects it. Version with the system.

1. **Intended use and context of use** - one sentence on the decision or record the system participates in, plus what it must not be used for. FDA draft guidance on AI supporting regulatory decision-making for drugs and biologics (Docket FDA-2024-D-4689, January 2025) proposes a risk-based credibility assessment framework in which the context of use defines the specific role and scope of the AI model in addressing a question of interest, and tailors oversight level, credibility-assessment stringency, performance acceptance criteria, risk mitigation, and documentation detail to the context of use and model risk. `CURRENT_PRIMARY` Draft guidance is current agency thinking, not binding, and was still draft when checked.
2. **Jurisdictions** - where data originates, where processing occurs, where output has effect. AI Act staging as enacted (Art 113): general application 2 August 2026; Chapters I-II, covering general provisions and prohibited practices, from 2 February 2025; GPAI, governance, and penalties chapters from 2 August 2025. The amending regulation known as the AI Omnibus, in force 27 July 2026, extended the high-risk obligations: Annex III standalone high-risk use cases to 2 December 2027, Annex I product-embedded high-risk to 2 August 2028. `CURRENT_PRIMARY` for the enacted Art 113 dates and the Commission's statement of the extended dates; the amending article numbering is `UNVERIFIED` here, so cite the date, not the clause. Prohibitions bite first, high-risk obligations last; engineers assume the reverse. Re-check these dates before designing to them - this timeline has already been amended once.
3. **Data classes** - personal, special-category, protected health information, cardholder account data or sensitive authentication data, GxP records, financially material records. A class you cannot name you cannot scope.
4. **Decision impact on a person** - a decision based solely on automated processing with legal or similarly significant effect engages GDPR Art 22(1); where it proceeds on the Art 22(2)(a) contract or 22(2)(c) explicit-consent basis, the Art 22(3) safeguards attach. Either way REG-05 is mandatory. `CURRENT_PRIMARY`
5. **Official records** - which artifacts become regulated records, signed records, financial-reporting inputs, inspection evidence. Sets REG-02 retention and REG-05 approval; usually discovered late.
6. **Obligation owner per regime** - a named role, not a department.

Output: an applicability frame naming each candidate regime, whether it applies, the obligation it creates here, the control addressing it, and the owner. Record the reasoning behind a not-applicable verdict; AI Act Art 6(4) requires a provider claiming an Annex III system is not high-risk to document that assessment before market placement. `CURRENT_PRIMARY`

## REG-02 - Evidence and Audit Trail

An audit trail is not a debug log. Required properties, with the instrument demanding each:

| Property | Requirement | Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Computer-generated | Secure, computer-generated, time-stamped, independently recording operator entries and actions that create, modify, or delete records | Part 11 11.10(e) |
| Append-only | Record changes shall not obscure previously recorded information | Part 11 11.10(e) |
| Actor identity | Signature unique to one individual, never reused or reassigned | Part 11 11.100(a) |
| Before/after and reason | Risk-based expectation: a system-generated record of GMP-relevant changes and deletions, the reason documented, convertible to a generally intelligible form, regularly reviewed | Annex 11 §9 |
| Human-readable retrieval | Accurate complete copies in human-readable and electronic form for inspection and copying; ready retrieval throughout retention | Part 11 11.10(b), 11.10(c) |
| Retention | At least as long as the subject records; six years for HIPAA documentation from creation or last effective date, whichever is later; deployer logs at least six months unless other Union or national law, in particular data-protection law, provides otherwise | Part 11 11.10(e); HIPAA 164.316(b)(2)(i); AI Act Art 26(6) |
| Independence and review | Audit controls a required standard with no implementation specification, plus regular review of audit logs and access reports | HIPAA 164.312(b), 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(D) |
| Lifetime event recording | Automatic recording of events over the system lifetime, sufficient to identify risk situations and substantial modifications and to support post-market and deployer monitoring | AI Act Art 12 |

All rows `CURRENT_PRIMARY`. PCI DSS has a logging-and-monitoring requirement family and the PCI SSC glossary defines an audit log as an independently verifiable trail sufficient to permit reconstruction `CURRENT_PRIMARY`; its sub-requirement numbers are `UNVERIFIED` here, so cite none.

Engineering consequences `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS`: emit from the code path performing the effect - a line an LLM composed is model output, not a trail. Correction appends; history does not move. Make entries tamper-evident by hash chain or WORM with a scheduled verification job, not a claim. Record the clock source and its synchronisation state; a timestamp from an unsynchronised container is not evidence of sequence. Give the audit store separate credentials so the identity performing an effect cannot delete the record of it. Schedule the Annex 11 §9 review with a named owner - the usual omission. An agent needs its own non-human identity, scoped per tenant and resource, and may never carry a person's signature.

Additional fields when the actor is a model `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS`:

| Field | Why required |
|---|---|
| Model and provider version | Reconstruction impossible without it; a provider-side change is a REG-03 event |
| Prompt, policy, tool-definition versions | Executable behaviour, not config |
| Tool calls with arguments and results | The effects actually attempted, in order |
| Retrieved evidence IDs and snapshot ID | Which corpus state produced the claim |
| Authority acted under | Delegating human, role, scope, approval reference, expiry |
| Reason code | Annex 11 §9 wants the reason, not just the diff |

Output: event schema, immutability and retention mechanism per record class, reviewer and cadence, and a demonstrated human-readable export of artifact plus manifest.

## REG-03 - Validation and Change Control While Shipping

Part 11 11.10(a) demands validation for accuracy, reliability, consistent intended performance, and ability to discern invalid or altered records; Annex 11 §11 demands periodic evaluation confirming the system remains in a valid state; Annex 11 §10 demands changes, including configuration, only in a controlled manner. `CURRENT_PRIMARY` A hosted model can change beneath you with no change record on your side, and stochastic output is not bitwise reproducible.

1. **Scale validation to risk, and write the risk assessment.** Annex 11 §1 makes the extent of validation and data-integrity controls depend on a justified, documented risk assessment. FDA final Computer Software Assurance guidance (Docket FDA-2022-D-0795, September 2025) applies risk-based assurance to computers and automated data processing systems used in medical-device production or the quality system, states software testing alone is often insufficient to establish confidence that software is fit for its intended use, and supersedes section 6 of *General Principles of Software Validation*. `CURRENT_PRIMARY` Its scope is the device frame; outside it the risk-based reasoning transfers as argument, not as applicable guidance. `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS` The lever against a demand for exhaustive scripts is that assessment, not more screenshots.
2. **Qualify what carries risk.** Traceable user requirements (Annex 11 §4.4); inventory of systems with their regulated functionality plus a system description for critical systems (§4.3); supplier competence and reliability treated as key selection factors with the need for an audit based on risk assessment (§3.2), and supplier quality-system and audit information made available to inspectors on request (§3.4). `CURRENT_PRIMARY` For a hosted model, supplier assessment is the only lever on internals.
3. **Frozen evaluation sets are the validation evidence.** Versioned immutable cases with pre-declared thresholds; a passing run at a pinned model version is the record of consistent intended performance. AI Act Art 9(8) expects testing against prior defined metrics and probabilistic thresholds appropriate to the intended purpose; Art 15(3) expects declared accuracy levels and metrics in the instructions for use. `CURRENT_PRIMARY` Build via EVA-01, score via EVA-02; the regulated additions are immutability, pre-declaration, and retention of the run record. `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS`
4. **Pin versions; a provider version change is a change-control event.** Contractual or API-level pinning plus a frozen-eval regression gate is the practical bridge between a validated state and a vendor-updated model. `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS` - a defensible pattern, not a cited allowance.
5. **Pre-declare the change envelope.** FDA final guidance on predetermined change control plans for AI-enabled device software functions (Docket FDA-2022-D-2628, December 2024) recommends that a PCCP describe the planned modifications, the methodology to develop, validate, and implement them, and an assessment of their impact; FDA reviews the PCCP within a marketing submission so that modifications inside it need no further submission. `CURRENT_PRIMARY` The only regulator-sanctioned "change the model without re-filing" pattern here, and it lives in the device frame; no equivalent exists for a GxP application whose upstream model is vendor-versioned. Borrow the structure as internal change control. `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS`
6. **Define what forces revalidation, before the first change.** Minimum triggers: model or provider version, prompt or policy, tool definition or permission, retrieval corpus or index rebuild, intended use or context of use, threshold, scheduled periodic evaluation. Record per trigger whether re-qualification is full, scoped, or regression-only, with justification. `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS`
7. **Controlled documentation.** Part 11 11.10(k)(2) requires revision and change-control procedures that themselves maintain a time-sequenced audit trail of systems documentation. 21 CFR 211.68(b) permits changes to master production and control records only by authorised personnel and requires input/output accuracy checks at a degree and frequency based on system complexity and reliability. AI Act Art 11 with Annex IV requires technical documentation drawn up before market placement and kept up to date. `CURRENT_PRIMARY`
8. **Guard against self-contamination.** AI Act Art 15(4) requires systems that keep learning after deployment to be built to eliminate or reduce feedback loops where biased output influences future input. `CURRENT_PRIMARY` Model output re-entering the retrieval corpus is that loop.
9. **Segregate propose from apply.** ICFR 13a-15(a) obliges covered issuers to maintain internal control over financial reporting, defined in 13a-15(f) to include policies and procedures providing reasonable assurance that receipts and expenditures occur only in accordance with authorisations of management and directors (13a-15(f)(2)), with annual management evaluation of effectiveness against a suitable recognised framework (13a-15(c)) and quarterly evaluation of any change that materially affected or is reasonably likely to materially affect ICFR (13a-15(d)). `CURRENT_PRIMARY` One service principal that both drafts and posts destroys segregation of duties whatever the prompt says: two identities, distinct authorisation, both recorded. `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS`

Output: intended-use and context-of-use record, risk assessment justifying validation depth, frozen eval set with thresholds and its passing run, revalidation-trigger table, periodic-evaluation job with an owner, and one change record per change linking code, prompts, policies, tools, indexes, and thresholds.

## REG-04 - Data Protection and Minimisation

The cheapest control is the data you never send.

1. **Classify before designing the data path.** Every field entering a prompt, trace, memory store, or index carries a class; unclassified defaults to the most restrictive class present in the source. `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS`
2. **Keep regulated data out of scope - the primary lever.** The PCI SSC glossary defines the cardholder data environment as the components, people, and processes that store, process, or transmit cardholder data or sensitive authentication data, plus components with unrestricted connectivity to them, and segmentation as isolating in-scope from out-of-scope systems. `CURRENT_PRIMARY` An inference service that never receives account data and cannot reach systems holding it stays out of the CDE; exclusion is cheaper than assessing an in-scope inference stack. `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS`
3. **Minimum necessary by design.** GDPR Art 5(1)(c) minimisation, 5(1)(e) storage limitation, Art 25(1) data protection by design naming pseudonymisation, Art 25(2) by default only necessary data across amount, extent of processing, storage period, and accessibility. HIPAA 164.514(d) requires identifying who needs what and limiting access accordingly; 164.514(d)(5) forbids use, disclosure, or request of an entire medical record unless specifically justified. `CURRENT_PRIMARY` Pasting a whole document into a prompt is the engineering shape of that entire-record request; scope the extract instead. `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS`
4. **De-identify, and know the limit.** HIPAA 164.514(b)(2) Safe Harbor requires removing the enumerated identifier classes - names, geography below state subject to the three-digit ZIP population rule, all date elements except year with ages over 89 collapsed, contact identifiers, record and account numbers, device and vehicle identifiers, URLs and IP addresses, biometric identifiers, full-face images, and any other unique identifying number, characteristic, or code - **and** no actual knowledge that residual information could re-identify. 164.514(b)(1) allows expert determination where a qualified person documents methods and results showing very small risk; 164.514(c) permits a re-identification code only where it is not derived from or related to information about the individual and is not otherwise translatable to identify them. `CURRENT_PRIMARY` Free text defeats field-level redaction; treat narrative as re-identifiable until measured. `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS`
5. **Strip or tokenise before the model call.** Prompt-and-response traces are the accidental second copy of everything: either treat the trace store as a system of the same classification with its own access review and retention, or store hashes and IDs instead of payloads. Fix inference, trace, index, and evaluation-data residency separately; they default apart. `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS`
6. **Encryption, stated correctly.** GDPR Art 32(1) names pseudonymisation and encryption among appropriate measures and Art 32(1)(d) requires a process for regularly testing, assessing, and evaluating effectiveness. HIPAA treats encryption at rest (164.312(a)(2)(iv)) and in transit (164.312(e)(2)(ii)) as **addressable**, not required: 164.306(d)(3) requires assessing whether the specification is reasonable and appropriate, implementing it if so, and otherwise documenting why not and implementing an equivalent alternative measure. `CURRENT_PRIMARY` The finding in practice is neither encrypting nor documenting.
7. **Retention per record class, not per store.** Map audit event, trace payload, eval result, training corpus, model artifact, and memory record each to a retention basis, period, and owner; one physical log with one TTL will violate something. Make that map the GDPR Art 30(1) record of processing, which must already carry envisaged erasure time limits (30(1)(f)) and a description of Art 32 measures (30(1)(g)), rather than a separate document that drifts. `CURRENT_PRIMARY` for Art 30 content, `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS` for reusing it as the retention map.
8. **Erasure versus retention, named.** GDPR Art 17(1) obliges erasure without undue delay on the listed grounds; Part 11 11.10(e) obliges audit-trail retention for at least the record retention period and HIPAA 164.316(b)(2)(i) obliges six years. Art 17(3)(b) exempts processing necessary for compliance with a legal obligation under Union or Member-State law - a non-Union retention duty is not automatically that obligation. `CURRENT_PRIMARY` **Resolution:** keep the identifier out of the immutable record. Write a pseudonymous subject key into the audit event; hold the key-to-identity mapping in a separate deletable store with its own access control. Erasure deletes the mapping, retention keeps the event. Verify the mapping store is not itself a regulated record before promising this, and record residual re-identification risk. `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS`
9. **Special-category data for bias measurement is a narrow door.** AI Act Art 10(5) permits it for bias detection and correction only where synthetic or anonymised data cannot achieve it, with re-use limits, documented strict access controls, no onward transfer, and deletion once bias is corrected or retention ends. `CURRENT_PRIMARY` Treat those as an implementation checklist and record the reason in the Art 30 record.

Output: field-level classification and data-flow map, scope-exclusion argument per regulated class, de-identification method with its residual-risk statement, per-class retention map serving as the processing record, and the pseudonymous-key design where erasure and retention collide.

## REG-05 - Human Authority and Official Output

A model may propose. It may never grant itself authority.

1. **Declare which outputs become official.** Per output type: regulated record, signed record, financial-reporting input, or internal draft. Only the first three get the full gate; applying it to drafts trains reviewers to rubber-stamp. `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS`
2. **Gate before effect, in a state machine outside the model.** Persist proposal, evidence, reviewer identity, decision, meaning of the decision, and timestamp as separate records. Part 11 11.50 requires a signed record to show the printed name of the signer, the date and time of execution, and the meaning of the signature - review, approval, responsibility, or authorship - in any human-readable rendering; 11.10(j) requires written policies holding individuals accountable for actions under their signature. `CURRENT_PRIMARY` "Approved" with no recorded meaning is not a signature.
3. **Show the reviewer enough to be informed.** AI Act Art 14 requires oversight enabling the assigned person to understand capacities and limitations, monitor for anomalies, remain aware of automation bias, interpret output correctly, disregard, override, or reverse it, and interrupt via a stop button or similar procedure halting the system in a safe state; Art 26(2) requires the deployer to assign oversight to persons with competence, training, and authority. `CURRENT_PRIMARY` Minimum reviewer packet: exact proposed artifact or action, destination, source evidence with locators, what was generated versus reproduced, model and prompt versions, declared limitations. A safe-state stop means checkpointing, not process kill; see HRN-02. `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS`
4. **Capture approve, edit, reject with identity and reason.** Store an edit as a distinct version, never a mutation of the proposal; the proposal-to-approved delta is the strongest quality signal available and feeds EVL-01. `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS` Annex 11 §12.4 requires recording the identity of operators who enter, change, confirm, or delete data with date and time; §12.3 requires recording creation, change, and cancellation of access authorisations. `CURRENT_PRIMARY`
5. **Four-eyes is not two-factor.** AI Act Art 14(5) requires, for certain biometric-identification systems, that no action be taken unless separately verified by at least two competent natural persons. Part 11 11.200(a)(1) requires two distinct identification components from one individual, and 11.200(a)(3) requires attempted misuse to need the collaboration of two or more individuals. Annex 11 §6 separately requires a second check on critical manually entered data, by a second operator or validated electronic means. `CURRENT_PRIMARY` MFA does not satisfy a two-person rule; a four-eyes workflow does not satisfy two-component signing.
6. **Human intervention on significantly-affecting decisions.** Where an Art 22 decision proceeds on the contract or explicit-consent basis, the GDPR Art 22(3) safeguards include at minimum the right to obtain human intervention on the controller's part, express a point of view, and contest the decision. `CURRENT_PRIMARY` Build all three as endpoints, not as policy text. A reviewer who cannot practically override is the automation bias AI Act Art 14(4)(b) names as a design target; measure override rate and time-per-review as evidence the gate is real. `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS`
7. **Approved-source retrieval for legally controlled text.** Where the exact wording of a text was approved, mandated, or certified by an accountable function, reproduce it verbatim from the approved source; never generate, paraphrase, summarise, or translate it. Implement as: an approved-source store with version and effective dates; retrieval by identifier, not similarity; byte-exact insertion; a validation step failing the output when a controlled block does not match its source hash; generation paths blocked for that content class. The model may select which approved block applies and compose the surrounding uncontrolled text. `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS` Related: Annex 11 §8.2 requires batch-release printouts to indicate if any data changed since original entry; Part 11 11.70 requires signatures linked to their records so they cannot be excised, copied, or transferred to falsify a record by ordinary means. `CURRENT_PRIMARY`
8. **Mark synthetic output.** AI Act Art 50(2) requires providers of systems generating synthetic audio, image, video, or text to mark output in a machine-readable format and make it detectable as artificially generated or manipulated. `CURRENT_PRIMARY` A generation-time product obligation, not a logging one.

Output: official-output register, approval state machine with terminal states, reviewer-packet specification, approve/edit/reject record schema, and the approved-source integrity check for controlled text.

## Where Regimes Conflict

Each needs a written, owned decision; none dissolves.

1. **Erasure versus retention.** GDPR Art 17(1) against Part 11 11.10(e) and HIPAA 164.316(b)(2)(i); resolve per REG-04 item 8.
2. **Append-only versus rectification.** Part 11 11.10(e) forbids obscuring prior information; GDPR Art 5(1)(d) requires inaccurate data rectified without delay. Compatible only as append-a-correction, where rectified means the current view is correct.
3. **AI Act log retention versus storage limitation.** Art 26(6) sets at least six months unless data-protection law provides otherwise; GDPR Art 5(1)(e) says no longer than necessary. The Act defers rather than resolves; write the chosen period and its justification.
4. **Observability instinct versus scope minimisation.** Full payload tracing manufactures a protected-health-information store, a CDE component once account data appears, and another processing activity. Decide payload retention explicitly; not by SDK default.
5. **Validated state versus a continuously updated model.** Only the device frame has a sanctioned answer (PCCP); elsewhere version pinning plus a frozen-eval regression gate, as `ENGINEERING_SYNTHESIS`.
6. **Risk-based validation versus exhaustive-script expectation.** FDA CSA and Annex 11 §1 permit risk-scaled effort; internal quality habit often does not. Cultural conflict; the lever is the written risk assessment plus the citations.
7. **Two-person verification versus two-factor signing.** AI Act Art 14(5) against Part 11 11.200(a)(1); REG-05 item 5.
8. **Bias remediation versus special-category restriction.** Measuring bias needs the attribute GDPR Art 9 restricts; AI Act Art 10(5) opens a conditional door with conditions to satisfy first.
9. **Autonomy versus human authority.** An agent closing the loop on a significantly-affecting decision collides with GDPR Art 22(1) absent a lawful basis plus Art 22(3) safeguards, and with the AI Act Art 14 requirement that the overseer can decide not to use the output.
10. **Agent service accounts versus segregation of duties.** Part 11 11.100(a) uniqueness against ICFR 13a-15(f)(2) authorisation; one identity that both proposes and executes satisfies neither.
